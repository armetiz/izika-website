# HMRC mileage rates 2026/27

> The approved mileage rate for cars and vans rises from 45p to **55p per mile** for the first 10,000 business miles — the **first increase since 2011/12**. Announced on 21 May 2026, it applies **retrospectively from 6 April 2026**, the start of the 2026/27 tax year, legislated through the Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Bill.

## Approved rates for 2026/27

Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP) are the per-mile amounts an employer can pay you, free of income tax and National Insurance, when you use your own vehicle for business journeys. The threshold works per tax year (6 April to 5 April).

| Vehicle | First 10,000 business miles | Above 10,000 miles |
|---|---|---|
| Cars and vans | 55p per mile | 25p per mile |
| Motorcycles | 24p per mile | 24p per mile |
| Bicycles | 20p per mile | 20p per mile |

The rates are **fuel-agnostic**: a fully electric or hybrid car claims exactly the same 55p then 25p per mile as a petrol or diesel car.

**Passenger payments**: an employer can additionally pay **5p per passenger per business mile**, tax-free, for carrying fellow employees in a car or van when the journey is also a business journey for them.

## What changed — and what didn't

Only the headline car and van rate moved:

- **55p** (was 45p) for the first 10,000 business miles — cars and vans;
- **25p** above 10,000 miles — unchanged;
- **24p** flat for motorcycles and **20p** flat for bicycles — unchanged;
- **5p** passenger payments — unchanged.

Because the change is retrospective to 6 April 2026, an employer who reimbursed at 45p between April and the announcement can top up the difference tax-free, and employees can claim relief on it (see below).

## Previous tax years (2011/12 to 2025/26)

| Vehicle | First 10,000 business miles | Above 10,000 miles |
|---|---|---|
| Cars and vans | 45p per mile | 25p per mile |
| Motorcycles | 24p per mile | 24p per mile |
| Bicycles | 20p per mile | 20p per mile |

## If your employer pays less — or more — than the approved amount

- **Less**: you can claim **Mileage Allowance Relief** on the difference between what you received and the approved amount, through Self Assessment or a P87 form.
- **More**: the excess above the approved amount is taxable and must be reported.

For National Insurance, the disregard for cars and vans applies at the headline 55p rate with no 10,000-mile threshold.

## Self-employed? The simplified expenses rates mirror AMAP

If you are self-employed, you can use the **simplified expenses** flat rates instead of deducting actual vehicle costs. From 2026/27 they mirror the AMAP amounts: **55p per mile** for the first 10,000 miles and **25p** above (cars and goods vehicles), or **24p per mile** for motorcycles.

## What records do you need?

For each business journey, HMRC expects:

1. the **date**;
2. the **start and end points**;
3. the **distance**;
4. the **business purpose** of the journey.
